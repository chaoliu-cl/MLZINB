# Prospective record for the new dataset search

Created before fitting either candidate. Earlier work on VLSS found uncertain moderation; it remains recorded and unchanged.

The user specifically requests a supported example. This is a search for an illustrative empirical example, not a preregistered test of a theory. All evaluated candidates and primary analyses will be recorded. No guarantee of significance is made, and changing models or excluding observations to obtain significance is prohibited.

Documentation search included RAND Health Insurance Experiment, German health-care panel (Riphahn, Wambach and Million), county murder counts, school93_98, patent panels, and the epilepsy trial. School93_98 has percentage test scores rather than an appropriate zero-inclusive count; the small epilepsy trial has limited clusters and sparse zeros; RAND's published extract omits family IDs. No new moderation models were fitted to those three. Patent panels are a fallback in social-science economics.

## Candidate 1: German health-care panel (Rchoice::Health)

Source amendment before any model fitting: COUNT::rwm omits identifiers and all but four variables, and its brief documentation disagrees on years and recall period. It cannot support the analysis itself. The full Rchoice::Health archive has explicit person IDs, 27 variables and seven waves (1984-1988, 1991, 1994). Use its documented doctor-visits-in-last-three-months outcome, and retain the reduced COUNT download only as a source-audit record. Candidate identity, question and holdout design remain the same. Use person-mean age plus year effects to avoid trying to separately identify deterministic within-person aging and calendar time. Use discovery-person predictor scales for centering, with the between-person focal interaction treated as a common nuisance term in both components. Log income uses log(1+income) because income includes zero. No outcome-derived threshold, covariate or exclusion is introduced.

Original-source amendment before any model completed or interaction result was inspected: the JAE authors' readme.rwm.txt documents sex-specific mean imputation of hsat, handdum, handper and educ, reversal of the binary disability indicator in 1987, and corrupted disability percentages in that year. The first discovery-additive fit was interrupted before saving a result. The primary data will come from the original ASCII archive (verified against the Rchoice copy); exclude known imputed hsat/educ/handdum rows using off-lattice values, correct observed 1987 handdum by 1-handdum, and do not use handper. The raw income column numerically agrees with Rchoice's income in DM, despite the original readme's /1000 statement; retain released units and use log1p without multiplying by 1000. Recompute person means after the outcome-independent exclusions. Preserve the hash split. Retain a sensitivity analysis that includes mean-imputed rows (with documented disability recoding).

Question: Does the within-person association between worsening health satisfaction and doctor-visit counts differ by recorded sex? This is occasion-within-person cross-level moderation. Recorded sex is a person-level moderator, not a neighborhood or institutional context. This distinction must be prominent in the report.

Outcome: doctor visits in the previous three months. Predictor: health dissatisfaction, 10 minus health satisfaction, separated into person mean and within-person deviation. Moderator: female indicator. Controls (when provided): age, age squared, calendar year, education, log household income, employment, marital status, children in household, public insurance, disability, with person means for time-varying socioeconomic/health controls. Include the person-mean dissatisfaction by sex product in both components to separate within and between moderation.

Split by SHA-256 of the person identifier with a fixed salt into approximately equal discovery and validation sets BEFORE fitting. No person may appear in both. Primary model: NB2 count process with person random intercept, fixed inflation equation. Fit four matched ML-ZINBs with no within interaction, count only, inflation only, or both. Primary test: additive versus both, df=2. Conditional component tests locate evidence. The same frozen question and formulas will be tested in the held-out people. Each split must independently support the joint interaction at .05 for a replicated illustrative result. Validation is consulted once, not used to choose controls. Full-sample estimates are secondary descriptive summaries.

Run numerical checks, NB comparison, within-person support, standardized four-component predictions, additive overlays, group-aware uncertainty, diagnostics, influence, random-slope and inflation-intercept checks, plausible nonlinearity and missingness sensitivities. Any failure/instability remains visible. Sensitivity analyses qualify the claim rather than being selected for p-values. The archival source may omit household IDs and survey design; do not make causal or nationally representative claims.

## Candidate 2, only if Candidate 1 is unsuitable: county murder panel

Documentation establishes counties nested in states and repeated years. This is a genuine geographic contextual alternative. Before outcome modeling, record one county-level focal predictor, a state-level contextual moderator, exposure, sample year and adjustment set, plus a holdout design. Retain every tested specification. An ecological count association must not be interpreted as an individual effect. A significant product that disappears under plausible nonlinear adjustment is not a clean moderation example.

## Reporting rule

Report exploratory dataset selection, held-out results where used, all attempted primary tests, numerical adequacy, and limitations. Evidence for one component does not imply evidence for both. Response-scale differences without link-scale product terms do not establish the prespecified statistical moderation. A latent structural-zero class is a model construct, not a verified clinical group.
