# CCD and NNCS structural refits

Specified before fitting on October 14, 2026. Existing data and previous results will be preserved.

Primary CCD: regular districts, types 1 and 2, positive membership and schools, state minimum 10 eligible districts reapplied, Vermont excluded because ELL counts are reported at supervisory-union centers. Context is common unweighted state rural-district share, standardized over distinct states. Focal predictor is rural status minus state share. Include a quadratic between-state context term so recentering does not force a previously implicit between-state interaction into the within-state coefficient. Adjust for log enrollment, log schools, city, small district, one school, and type-2 status. Enrollment is the count offset. Use state random intercepts in count and inflation components.

Primary NNCS: reproduce the 2010-2013 complete-annual-count sample and exclusions for fractional counts, retaining 6,849 city-specific tract/segment records. Split disadvantage into within-city deviation and city mean. Standardize deviation using its pooled SD, city means and Gini over distinct cities, and the three original tract controls over records. Adjust for partial-tract status. Include city mean disadvantage by Gini in both components as a distinct between-city association, so within-city moderation is not forced to equal between-city moderation. Use city random intercepts in count and inflation components and the existing population-times-four count offset.

For each primary analysis, fit additive, within interaction in count only, within interaction in inflation only, and within interaction in both components. Compare these four specifications on exactly the same records. Report both conditional interaction tests from the joint model and nested likelihood-ratio tests. Retain the negative-binomial-2 count family. Fit an NB comparator on each primary sample.

CCD planned joint-model checks: including Vermont; independent type-1 districts only; linear between-state context; independent state random slopes in the count component; a count-component union effect applying only to union components; and the previous small-district moderator as a secondary application.

NNCS planned joint-model checks: fixed inflation without city random intercept; metro count random intercept added to city effects; whole tracts; independent city random slopes in the count component; additive between-city disadvantage/context relationship; and within/between decomposition of the three additional tract controls. Regional identifiers stay at METROID2, matching the supplied regional definitions.

These are targeted refits of previously selected moderators, not a new moderator screen. Nominal p-values do not remove the original exploratory selection. AIC comparisons are restricted to models fitted to the same outcome records. Report singular/boundary fits, numerical failures, and imprecise inflation coefficients. If an optimizer fails or leaves an appreciable gradient, retry the identical specification with BFGS and retain a numerically acceptable optimum when available. Do not simplify a failed model silently.

No reassignment of Vermont union ELL totals to districts, new geographic crosswalk, or annual panel will be fabricated. Those designs require additional reporting or exposure information.

Numerical implementation clarification: the initial runs used an unscaled gradient cutoff, which depends on the likelihood and parameter scales. An independent audit of the completed joint fits found Newton corrections below 0.003 standard-error units; strict warm refinement of the CCD joint fit changed its coefficients negligibly. Final numerical acceptance uses optimizer code zero, a positive-definite Hessian, maximum absolute Newton correction below 0.01 parameter standard errors, and Newton decrement below 0.01. Raw gradients remain reported. Initial attempts/checkpoints are retained, and the criterion for each optimizer-attempt record is identified. Remaining fits use completed joint-model estimates as starting values when compatible; model formulas and samples are unchanged.

Additional design check specified after the CCD count random-slope model improved AIC by approximately 204 points: fit a joint model with independent state intercepts and within-state rural slopes in both count and inflation components. This checks whether the inflation interaction persists when residual state slope variation is allowed in that component too. It is an additional structural assessment, not a newly selected moderator.

For the two NNCS reduced fits omitting the count interaction, default optimizers and a profiled retry were numerically problematic. A final equivalent parameterization maps the omitted interaction coefficient(s) to zero in the full joint formula. Numerical bounds on log random-effect SDs (-5 to 2), log dispersion (-5 to 5), and fixed coefficients (-30 to 30) prevent extreme trial points; active bounds are flagged. These are optimizer safeguards, not a basis for accepting an invalid Hessian. Failed candidates and logs are retained, and no failed candidate supplies a valid nested test.
